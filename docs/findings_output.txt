Findings as shown on the Executive Overview
================================================================================

Q: Is daily revenue growing? 
Result: Daily revenue grew by about 17.9% per month (95% CI 16.0% to 19.9%); mean daily revenue went from $2,635 in Jan to $5,550 in Jun. 
Method: Log-linear regression of daily revenue on day plus weekday, Newey-West standard errors (181 days). 
Caveat: Six months cannot separate growth from seasonality (winter to summer), and the source is a sample dataset. 

Q: How concentrated is demand in the morning? 
Result: 45.8% of revenue falls between 07:00 and 10:59 (95% CI 43.6% to 48.0%): 4 of the 15 trading hours. By store: Astoria 38.5%, Hell's Kitchen 48.2%, Lower Manhattan 50.7%. 
Method: Revenue share by hour; bootstrap interval resampling whole days (181 days, 2,000 resamples). 
Caveat: Opening hours differ by store (Astoria opens later), so store shares partly reflect when each store is open. Sales show realised demand, not queue length or lost sales; a staffing change should be trialled and measured (e.g. wait times, sales per labour hour). 

Q: Do weekends sell more than weekdays? 
Result: Weekend days averaged -0.1% revenue relative to weekdays of the same week (95% CI -5.0% to +5.0%, p = 0.958): no detectable difference. 
Method: Regression of log daily revenue on week fixed effects, a linear trend and a weekend indicator (25 weeks), so growth does not masquerade as a weekend effect. 
Caveat: Assumes the weekend effect is the same every week; with ~50 weekend days, differences smaller than a few percent cannot be ruled out. 

Q: Do the stores perform differently? 
Result: Daily revenue differences between stores are within 2.8% of each other (smallest Holm-adjusted p = 0.256). Average basket value is highest at Lower Manhattan ($6.45) and lowest at Astoria ($5.71), but store explains only 0.35% of basket-value variance. 
Method: Paired t-tests on daily revenue (paired by date, Holm correction); Welch ANOVA and eta squared for basket value. 
Caveat: No data on foot traffic, opening hours, floor space or costs, so this compares sales, not efficiency or profit. 

Q: Which products are bought together? 
Result: Ouro Brasileiro shot → Ginger Scone: 31.3% of baskets with Ouro Brasileiro shot also contain Ginger Scone, versus 2.0% of all baskets (lift 15.8, 688 baskets). 24.8% of baskets contain 2+ products. 
Method: Apriori over 116,790 store+second baskets (support >= 0.2%, confidence >= 10%), Fisher exact test with Holm adjustment. 
Caveat: Sold together at 2 of 3 selected stores; within-store lift 15.4 (Hell's Kitchen), 13.0 (Lower Manhattan), so the pattern is not just where products are stocked. Baskets are inferred, not true orders. Association is not causation; test a bundle before rolling it out.
