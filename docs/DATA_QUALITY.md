# Data quality report

Source file: `Dashboard/Coffee Shop Sales.xlsx` (149,116 rows × 11 columns, 2023-01-01 to 2023-06-30).
The workbook is never modified. Cleaning happens in `Dashboard/lib/data.R` (`clean_sales()`), and the
results below are regenerated by `Rscript analysis/findings.R` (see `docs/findings_output.txt`).

## Rules

- **Hard rules:** rows failing them are excluded from the analysis set and counted. They cover a
  missing required field, an unparseable timestamp, quantity ≤ 0 or fractional, and unit price ≤ 0.
- **Soft rules:** rows are kept and flagged. The flags are visible and filterable in the Data
  Explorer and included in the CSV export.
- Nothing is imputed. Text fields are trimmed of surrounding whitespace.

## Notes on the flagged rows

**Near-duplicates (398).** These rows match an earlier row in everything except the ID: same store,
second, product, quantity and price. In 96.5% of the groups the IDs are within 3 of each other, so
they look like the same product rung up on separate lines of one basket. Double entry can't be ruled
out. They are kept because removing them would be a guess either way. For basket analysis each
product counts once per basket, so the near-duplicates don't affect support, confidence or lift.

**Non-standard prices (1,262 lines, 15 products).** Examples:
- Bakery items at a second price point at Lower Manhattan (439 lines) and Hell's Kitchen (23 lines).
  These fall on the 9th and from the 20th to the end of every month, a recurring calendar pattern
  that suggests scheduled pricing rather than random entry errors.
- Ouro Brasileiro shot at $2.10 and at $3.00.
- Branded merchandise at two price points.

These could be price changes, promotions or artefacts of how the sample was generated. No correction
is applied.

**High quantities (36 lines).** Examples:
- Civet Cat, 8 × $45 = $360 per line.
- Syrups at 4 × $0.80.

Several recur at identical clock times on the same day of different months, e.g. Civet Cat at
09:55:47 on the 17th of January, April and June. That pattern suggests templated records rather than
real purchases. They are kept, but they inflate per-line outliers in the price charts.

**Spelling in the source.** "Jamacian Coffee River" (beans) vs "Jamaican Coffee River Lg" (drinks),
"Columbian", "Carmel syrup". These are kept as in the source because product IDs are consistent.

**Opening hours differ by store** (first and last sale across the period): Astoria 07:00–19:59,
Hell's Kitchen 06:30–20:59, Lower Manhattan 06:00–20:28. Hourly comparisons between stores partly
reflect this.

**Time zone.** Times are store-local clock times. They are stored in UTC purely as a container, so a
viewer's time zone or daylight saving cannot shift them.
